ExoSett project role

Site office

Keeping the project organised, coordinated and properly recorded.

The Site office is the administrative and coordination function for an ExoSett project. It maintains the controlled project record, keeps responsibilities and approvals visible, coordinates external work and preserves continuity from project establishment through operation, change and eventual decommissioning. It does not replace the designers, Safety officer or other specialists responsible for technical work.

What they bring

Order, continuity and follow-through

A Site office brings disciplined administration, document control, procurement and delivery coordination, and the ability to keep a complex project moving without obscuring who is responsible for each decision. It may be a team dedicated to one project, a department supporting several projects or an external specialist organisation.

The people providing the function need enough understanding of the project to recognise gaps, dependencies and missing evidence. Their authority is administrative: they pursue answers, convene the right people and police the state of the record, but do not settle engineering or safety questions themselves.

Responsibilities

Coordinate the project

The Site office helps the Project client identify, interview and appoint people or organisations for the specialist project roles. It establishes the project’s administrative infrastructure and supports meetings, correspondence, decisions, approvals and routine records.

Throughout the project, it:

  • keeps responsibilities, dependencies and outstanding actions visible;
  • notices work that is falling between roles and gets its ownership resolved;
  • brings the appropriate specialists together where responsibilities overlap;
  • records the resulting decisions, approvals and changes;
  • tracks programme, procurement, manufacture, delivery and project progress; and
  • maintains the records needed to understand what was specified and what was actually built.

The building specification

Control the authoritative project record

The Site office owns the administration of the building specification. It keeps the specification in good order and under version control, with a clearly identifiable current state, a traceable change history and an explicit approval state for its contents. Approved, provisional and awaiting-approval material must be distinguishable wherever that distinction matters.

The Site office actively polices the approval state of the specification. The record should show who is responsible for each specialist part, who approved it and which version governed a procurement, manufacture or construction decision. It should be possible to establish the approved specification at any point in the project’s history without relying on personal recollection.

Administrative control is not technical approval. The Frame designer, for example, creates and signs off the technical frame specification; the Site office ensures that the contribution exists, is correctly versioned and carries the required approval. The same principle applies to the Building designer, Module designer, Services designer, Foundation designer, Roof designer and Safety officer within their respective domains.

Coordination and procurement

Turn approved decisions into coordinated work

The Site office coordinates invitations to bid, supplier evaluation, appointments and contracts for frame materials, accommodation modules, other building components and project services. It tracks procurement, manufacture and delivery, and coordinates contractors and suppliers during construction, testing and handover.

Coordination does not transfer decision-making authority. Technical selection remains with the relevant specialist; commercial commitment remains with the Project client or another properly authorised party. The Site office administers the process and makes sure its inputs, decisions and approvals are complete and recorded.

During delivery, it coordinates logistics, sequencing, services work, testing and handover between the responsible parties. This includes accommodation-module transport, arrival, installation and later movement, without making the Site office the designer of the modules, frames, lifting arrangements or building services.

Through the building life cycle

Preserve knowledge as the building changes

The Site office continues after initial construction. It preserves the authoritative project and building records, supports inspections, maintenance and repair, and establishes and administers maintenance programmes through appropriately competent people and organisations.

It coordinates later changes and ensures that each one is reviewed by the appropriate specialists, approved where required and reflected in the building specification. This includes the installation, removal, replacement or refurbishment of accommodation modules and the records needed for safe future decisions.

At the end of the building’s useful life, the Site office coordinates decommissioning and disassembly, including the appropriate reuse, recovery or disposal of components. The controlled record preserves the history and evidence needed by those who plan and carry out that work.

Interfaces

Keep responsibilities clear

The Site office helps every participant understand their responsibilities, remain within their authority and resolve interfaces with the other appropriate roles. It offers administrative help while reminding each specialist that they remain responsible for creating and signing off the information within their domain.

Where an issue has no clear owner, the Site office identifies the gap, brings together the people able to allocate or resolve it and records the outcome. It does not silently adopt the technical responsibility or resolve an engineering or safety question by administrative decision.
