ExoSett project role

Safety officer

Keeping the building safe and appropriately approved throughout its life.

The Safety officer coordinates safety assurance, regulatory approvals and certifications from design and manufacture through operation, alteration and eventual deconstruction. The role makes sure that the necessary safety questions are answered and appropriately evidenced; it does not replace the designers or specialists responsible for answering them.

What they bring

Experience, judgement and authority

A Safety officer brings:

  • experience administering safety, compliance, approval or certification processes on substantial construction projects;
  • broad technical understanding and the judgement to recognise when specialist expertise is needed;
  • knowledge of the applicable local regulatory regime and experience working with regulators, inspectors, certification bodies and specialist professionals;
  • disciplined management of evidence and records, supported by clear communication; and
  • the authority and professional confidence to require missing safety assurance to be resolved before work progresses.

The role does not require one person to hold every specialist engineering qualification. The Project client, together with the Site office, ensures that an appropriately competent person always occupies the role, even when the person changes during the building’s life.

Responsibilities

Coordinate and assure building safety

The Safety officer identifies the requirements that apply, brings in the necessary specialists and authorities, and coordinates the evidence, inspections, regulatory approvals and certifications. Depending on the project and its location, this includes:

  • fire safety, structural safety and building-services safety;
  • manufacture, transport, lifting, installation and temporary construction conditions;
  • safe occupation, access, operation and maintenance;
  • safety-related environmental compliance and other regulated specialist systems; and
  • alteration, module removal, deconstruction and demolition.

The breadth of the role changes with the building and the local regulatory regime. Its purpose is to find the questions that must be answered, not to make the Safety officer the technical expert responsible for every answer.

Interfaces

Assure the design; support safe delivery

The designers design. The Safety officer makes sure that their safety responsibilities have been identified, addressed by appropriately competent people and supported by suitable evidence. The Building designer retains responsibility for overall design coordination, while the Frame designer, Module designer, Services designer and Foundation designer retain responsibility for the work appropriate to their roles.

The Safety officer defines, coordinates and assures safety requirements and processes. The Site office implements those processes in practice during manufacture, transport, construction, commissioning, operation, maintenance, alteration and deconstruction.

The building specification

Maintain a continuous assurance record

The Safety officer maintains the safety-assurance information associated with the building specification: the applicable requirement, the responsible designer or specialist, the evidence required, the relevant approving or certification body, the current status and references to the resulting records.

This record remains important after initial approval. Replacing modules, changing facades or services, adding floors or removing parts of the building can affect established safety assumptions and may require evidence, approvals or certifications to be revisited.

Industry context

How this role relates to industry practice

Safety responsibilities on conventional building projects are usually spread across several roles. Health and safety officers are often concerned primarily with operational and site safety. Principal designers coordinate safety and regulatory compliance during design, principal contractors manage safety during construction, and regulators, inspectors and certification bodies provide independent approvals.

The ExoSett Safety officer does not replace any of these specialists or authorities. Instead, the role gives continuing safety assurance a clearly named home across the building lifecycle: identifying the regulatory approvals and certifications required, ensuring that the appropriate competent people and authorities are involved, and recording the resulting evidence in the building specification.

The Site office remains responsible for implementing safety-related processes in practice.
