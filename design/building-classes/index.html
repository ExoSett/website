ExoSett design

Building classes and component records

Reconfigurable buildings need both reusable design requirements and reliable records of the buildings and components made to those designs. ExoSett distinguishes a building class from each physical building and each identifiable component.

These distinctions support component replacement, maintenance and reuse. They form the basis of ExoSett’s developing information model, whose XML prototype implements a limited part of the wider design intent.

Building class: the reusable design basis

A building class defines the reusable design requirements for buildings and their components. These can include geometry, dimensions, structural arrangements, component classes, design positions, interfaces, services, tolerances and rules. Here, “building class” names an ExoSett design concept, not a regulatory occupancy category.

A Building Class Document records these definitions or imports them from other class documents. Component classes belong within Building Class Documents. A document can cover a limited subject, such as a beam definition or pod interface, without defining an entire building. Combining such documents does not by itself establish a complete or approved design.

For example, a class could define pod dimensions and the interfaces required to fit an accommodation frame. It could also specify requirements for the proposed independent fire-stopping components. Those requirements would still need supporting engineering and evidence.

Building instance: one physical building

A building instance is a particular physical building. Its Building Instance Document identifies the class revision adopted as its design basis and records its own configuration: which components occupy its positions, site-specific information, modifications, assessed conformity, deviations and supporting approvals.

Several buildings can use the same design definitions while having different components, histories and site conditions. An empty position is different from one whose occupant is unknown; neither should be silently treated as a confirmed installation.

Component instance: one identifiable item

A component instance is an individual physical item, such as a pod or beam. Its Component Instance Document identifies the item and its class definition and revision, and records its manufacture, inspection, condition, installation, removal, storage, repair and reuse with supporting evidence.

Two components made to the same definition can have different histories and conditions. An item retains its identity when it leaves a building; its record can continue while it is in storage or undergoing repair. Retaining a class reference does not prove continued fitness for use.

Replacing a pod without losing its history

Consider a pod removed from a bay for refurbishment and replaced by another pod. The installation position in that building retains its identity while its occupant changes. The building record identifies the replacement and the evidence supporting its installation. The removed pod retains its own identity and history, and its component record follows its removal and refurbishment.

The building and component records need to agree about the change. Replacing a pod need not change the building class: the replacement’s suitability must be assessed against the adopted design requirements and the particular installation. This distinction supports reconfigurability and the independent lifecycles of pods, frames and other components.

Design revisions and engineering evidence

Issued document revisions are immutable: later changes or corrections create new revisions while preserving earlier records. A building adopts a specific class revision. Publishing a new design revision does not automatically change existing buildings or their design basis.

Design requirements, recorded physical condition and engineering approval are distinct. A valid XML document, a resolved reference or membership of a class does not establish compliance or suitability. Evidence and approvals must relate to the relevant design revision, physical component, configuration and intended use.
