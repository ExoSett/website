ExoSett design

Fire safety

Fire safety is a fundamental building-design responsibility. ExoSett does not make a building fireproof, and the accommodation frame cannot provide a complete fire strategy by itself.

An ExoSett building should use a layered fire-safety approach involving the accommodation pods, accommodation frame, service frame, façades, access routes and building services.

The appropriate strategy will be project-specific. The principles and possible measures described here are design considerations and options, not a finished ExoSett fire-safety product, and ExoSett does not itself guarantee regulatory compliance.

The safety officer oversees this building-wide responsibility and coordinates its interfaces with every contributing specialist.

Building-wide responsibility

A layered fire-safety approach

The detailed strategy will depend on the building’s height, use, layout, location and regulatory requirements, but should address the following principles:

  • Reduce foreseeable sources of ignition.
  • Detect fire and warn occupants promptly.
  • Control fire and smoke near their source.
  • Protect escape routes and areas of relative safety.
  • Maintain structural stability for the required period.
  • Prevent rapid and disproportionate spread between pods, bays, storeys and other parts of the building.
  • Provide suitable access, information and facilities for firefighters.
  • Support inspection, maintenance and safe recovery after a fire.

Components and interfaces

Pods and accommodation frame both contribute

Accommodation pods are expected to make a significant contribution. Depending on their use, this may include fire-resisting construction, controlled internal materials, detection, suppression, protected service penetrations and suitable doors and ventilation interfaces.

The accommodation frame also has an important role. Spaces around pods must not become uncontrolled routes through which smoke or flame can spread across the building. Possible measures may include:

  • fire and smoke barriers within the frame;
  • division of the frame into controlled fire or smoke zones;
  • protected service connections and penetrations;
  • fire-safe arrangements for empty bays;
  • smoke extraction or ventilation through defined routes;
  • fire protection for structural members and connections.

Research and development

Independent fire-stopping components

Fire protection within an ExoSett building could be provided by a combination of the fire resistance of individual pods and fire-stopping components associated with the accommodation frame.

One approach would be to install fire-stop pads within the faces of bays, sealing against the columns and beams rather than against the pods themselves. This could allow fire-stopping components to remain in place irrespective of whether neighbouring bays contain pods or are empty. The pads are envisaged as components of the accommodation frame, not as seals between adjacent pods.

Vacuum-compressed stone wool offers a possible means of installing such components. The FIRETIGHT Fire Stop from Alert Isolatie provides a commercial precedent: its vacuum-packed stone wool expands to fill a space when the wrapping is punctured following installation. This illustrates an existing technology; it does not mean that this product has been selected or shown to be suitable for ExoSett buildings.

The objective would be to make the installation, inspection and replacement of fire-stopping components independent of the installation and replacement of pods. Wherever practicable, decisions about one component’s lifecycle should be independent of decisions about the other’s.

The geometry and performance requirements of these components could be specified by the building class. The feasibility of this approach and the fire performance of the complete assembly, including structural connections and service penetrations, would need to be established through research and testing. This is an avenue for development, not an established ExoSett design solution or evidence of fire-safety compliance.

Engineered strategy

Manage fire and smoke across the building

Completely sealing every space is not necessarily the correct answer. Some buildings require controlled ventilation or smoke extraction. The essential requirement is that airflow, smoke movement and fire spread are understood and managed as part of an engineered building-wide fire strategy.

Every permitted configuration of an ExoSett building—including bays containing pods, empty bays and pod replacement—must maintain the level of fire safety required by the building design.
