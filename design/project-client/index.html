ExoSett project role

Project client

Defining why the building should exist and establishing the authority and financial basis to make it happen.

The Project client originates the proposition, sets its strategic requirements and commissions the work needed to turn that intent into a viable ExoSett building. The client does not personally perform the specialist design work.

What they bring

The knowledge behind the proposition

A Project client brings knowledge of:

  • the building’s value proposition, including its relationship to time and cost;
  • the functional goal the building must fulfil;
  • how its provision and operation could be funded;
  • the site on which it could be built;
  • the required delivery timeframe; and
  • the intended building life cycle.

The value proposition explains why the building is worth creating and provides a basis for judging cost, timing and emerging design choices. It is not necessarily fixed: circumstances can change, and the proposed building’s value may change with them—sometimes rapidly.

Responsibilities

Test the proposition before expanding the project

The Project client is responsible for:

  • engaging a Building designer;
  • working initially with the Building designer to determine whether the proposition is viable;
  • ensuring that the project has an adequate financial basis as it develops;
  • making the strategic decisions needed for design work to progress; and
  • establishing a Project office when the proposition is sufficiently viable to justify fuller development.

Initial proposition→viability exercise→decision to proceed→establish Project office

A full Project office or multidisciplinary team need not exist from the first day. Project formation can be fluid: design work may begin before implementation staff are in place, or a Site office may already exist when the Building designer joins. Responsibilities should follow the nature of the work—the Building designer leads design development and the Site office leads implementation planning—while early project staff work flexibly and keep assumptions, decisions and responsibilities explicit.

What they deliver

A clear expression of what the project must achieve

The client turns what they know into an agreed basis on which the rest of the project can act. It expresses:

  • the functional goal;
  • the site;
  • the delivery timeframe;
  • the value proposition and financial constraints;
  • the funding plan;
  • the life-cycle intent; and
  • strategic approvals and decisions as the project develops.

This describes the required outcomes and constraints. It does not prescribe the detailed technical solution, which is developed and verified by appropriately qualified project participants.

Early design

Launching the building definition

The Project client and Building designer begin with an abstract account of what the building must achieve. Together they give the first concrete form to the ExoSett building definition. At this stage it is exploratory: ideas are expected to be compared, challenged and changed.

Early work may explore:

  • accommodation-module dimensions;
  • the proposed number and arrangement of accommodation cells;
  • the required mix of accommodation units;
  • the number of modules needed to realise the emerging accommodation types;
  • the broad foundation approach; and
  • initial ideas for roofing and façades.

The client defines needs such as the accommodation mix. Technical consequences—such as the module arrangement needed to realise a dwelling type—emerge through design.

The client and Building designer can use the ExoSett Sketch app to explore and communicate scale, possible frame arrangements and accommodation capacity. It is an aid to early discussion, not a substitute for professional design.

Early cost checking

Test value, scope and cost together

Cost checking begins while the building idea is still fluid, not after its geometry has been developed. Using professional experience, precedent and available market pricing, the Building designer can produce an initial indicative cost. The client compares that emerging proposal with the value proposition, funding plan and available resources.

Need→initial building idea→indicative cost→revise scope or design→reassess

This loop may run many times. It tests not only whether the project can be afforded, but whether the evolving proposition remains worth pursuing as time, cost and circumstances change.

Shared project definition

Working with the building specification

The ExoSett building specification is the evolving definition of the building. The Project client is the ultimate owner of the project and its specification. Within the project, different roles initiate, author, maintain, review and approve the portions for which they are responsible. In that operational sense they own their portions, while overall ownership remains with the client. Specialist technical information must be completed and signed off by appropriately qualified participants. The Site office ultimately acts upon the approved specification.

The client initiates the functional goal, value proposition, site, timeframe, funding and life-cycle intent; contributes knowledge and constraints; reviews the emerging whole; and approves strategic decisions. The specification records this knowledge alongside the technical definition produced by the design team.

Information begins as exploratory and becomes progressively agreed and locked. The Building designer communicates design maturity and dependencies; the Site office communicates implementation, procurement, programme and construction consequences. Before a locked element is reopened, the client should understand the likely cost and effects on related decisions and completed work. Changing module dimensions late in a project, for example, could be very expensive.

The authoritative technical source for the specification format, schemas, examples and versioning guidance is the ExoSett modelling repository. Consult the current repository rather than relying on a schema description copied into this page.
