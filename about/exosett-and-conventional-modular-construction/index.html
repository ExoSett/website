About ExoSett

ExoSett and conventional modular construction

Conventional modular construction and ExoSett can both make use of factory-produced accommodation. The principal distinction lies in the role modularity is designed to play throughout the life of a building.

Modularity can be a means of constructing a building, but it can also be an enduring property of the building itself. ExoSett is designed around the latter principle.

Modularity throughout the life of a building

In many modular construction systems, factory-produced units are assembled to form a finished building. The resulting building may offer varying degrees of adaptability, depending on how it has been designed.

In an ExoSett building, the installation, removal and replacement of accommodation pods are anticipated from the outset as normal lifecycle activities. The accommodation is intended to evolve as the needs of its occupants change.

Separation of accommodation and infrastructure

ExoSett separates three principal elements of a building:

  • Accommodation pods, providing the spaces in which people live, work or undertake other activities.
  • The accommodation frame, providing structural support independently of the presence or absence of pods.
  • The service frame, providing access, circulation and utility infrastructure independently of the accommodation.

The accommodation frame is designed to remain structurally capable whether its bays contain pods or are empty. This separation is intended to allow pods to be replaced independently and to accommodate different maintenance and replacement cycles for accommodation and infrastructure.

Beyond the individual building

ExoSett distinguishes between a building class, which defines shared design requirements and component interfaces, and a building instance, which is a particular physical building using those requirements as its design basis.

Buildings of the same class can have different configurations and purposes while sharing compatible components. Common specifications provide a basis for components from different suppliers to be tested for conformance and assessed for use in particular buildings.

A pod removed from one building could be reused in another compatible building, subject to its condition and suitability for the new installation. Individual buildings can be constructed, reconfigured or dismantled while the shared building system continues to develop.

The ExoSett Value Proposition explores the potential long-term economic and environmental value of this approach.

Different approaches for different needs

ExoSett builds upon the principles of modular construction rather than seeking to replace them. Its particular focus is on circumstances where adaptability, component interchangeability and reuse have value throughout the life of a building and beyond.

ExoSett remains a developing building system. The practical realisation of these capabilities is subject to ongoing design, engineering and validation. Interchangeability across independent manufacturers has not yet been demonstrated; the reconfigurability page explains the specifications and verification work involved.
