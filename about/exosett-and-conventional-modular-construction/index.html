About ExoSett

ExoSett and conventional modular construction

Conventional modular construction and ExoSett both use factory-produced volumetric accommodation units, but they organise the relationship between accommodation, structure, access and services differently.

ExoSett is intended to place removable accommodation modules within an independent structural frame, with an associated service frame providing access, circulation and utility infrastructure. Conventional modular construction includes many different systems; this comparison describes common approaches rather than every modular building.

The central distinction

Separate roles within one building system

ExoSett separates the accommodation module, the accommodation frame that carries structural loads, and the associated service frame that supports access, circulation and utilities.

The accommodation frame is intended to remain structurally capable whether a cell contains a module or is temporarily empty. This makes change a defining design condition rather than an exceptional intervention.

Structure

Modules hosted by an independent frame

Many modular systems use modules as part of their structural arrangement. Others use separate frames, podiums, cores or hybrid structures. Conventional modular buildings do not all rely entirely on stacked modules.

ExoSett’s specific proposition is that accommodation modules should not be required to carry the building independently or support modules above them. Loads are intended to pass through a structure assembled from columns, beams and frame nodes, with temporary bracing used where the construction or alteration sequence requires it.

Installation and removal

Change requires a controlled process

Removing a module is not simply the reverse of placing it by crane. The module must be disconnected, supported, moved clear of the frame and transferred safely while the surrounding building remains protected and operational constraints are managed.

ExoSett anticipates controlled installation and removal using temporary equipment including an installation frame, a module jig and, where required, a frame jig. Their detailed forms, sequences and limits remain subjects of design and engineering work, rather than final engineering solutions.

Access and services

Infrastructure that remains available

The service frame is an essential associated part of an ExoSett building. It is intended to preserve access and circulation when modules change, while supporting the escape routes required by the particular building design.

Defined service routes and service interfaces are intended to keep utilities accessible and allow maintenance without opening occupied accommodation. Access bridges may connect the service frame to individual modules while accommodating the separation between the two structures.

Change over time

Adapt the accommodation while retaining useful infrastructure

ExoSett is intended to support replacement of damaged or obsolete modules, changes in dwelling or room size, and a changing accommodation mix. The evolving hotel and old prison stories explore how accommodation might change without replacing an entire building.

Capacity could be added through additional floors or reduced through downsizing, subject to the design and engineering of a particular project. Modules and structural components are intended to be reusable where their design, condition, transport and regulatory context permit.

Accessible, separable infrastructure is also important to this adaptability. The simple utilities story illustrates one possible approach to changing service connections over time.

Important qualification

What ExoSett does not claim

ExoSett is a developing building-system concept, not a proven commercial construction system. Detailed structural engineering and the practical methods needed to realise the system are still being developed.

Suitability will depend on site, height, loading, fire, acoustic, environmental, regulatory and operational requirements. Each implementation would need project-specific design, engineering and approval.

This comparison does not claim that ExoSett is universally superior. Conventional modular systems may be preferable where rapid delivery, established certification, standardised procurement or an existing supply chain is the main priority.

In summary

A building system designed around continued change

ExoSett is not defined merely by using removable modules. Its defining proposition is the combination of removable accommodation modules, an independent accommodation frame and an associated service frame intended to allow the building to change over time.
