# Why offsite construction isn’t working

## Source

- Slug: `2026-broadhead-offsite-construction`
- Author: James Broadhead, partner of IGV Housing and COO of IGV Build Systems
- Publication: Construction Business, REMI Network
- Publication date: 3 November 2026
- Original URL: https://www.reminetwork.com/articles/why-offsite-construction-isnt-working/
- Date accessed: 4 November 2026

## Synopsis

A practitioner critique of offsite delivery, advocating selective combinations of factory and site work.

## Main arguments and findings

- Production queues and premature design commitments can undermine delivery.
- Approval changes, tolerances and service coordination can cause rework.
- Factory expenditure can precede recognised lending milestones.
- Hybrid methods and early collaboration are proposed responses.

## Qualifications and limitations

This is industry opinion, not a controlled comparative study. The author's commercial roles are relevant context. Generalisations should not be treated as universal findings. The complete article was accessible; no access limitation was encountered.

## Relevance to ExoSett

The following is ExoSett’s interpretation. The source does not evaluate or endorse ExoSett.

Common frames, nodes and interfaces could reduce repeated design work while allowing independent suppliers to produce compatible accommodation. Paired accommodation and service frames could separate replacement cycles. Neither proposition eliminates capacity constraints, funding gaps or coordination responsibilities.

The XML model records arrangement; a fuller maintained specification and verified physical jigs could support conformity. Neither schema validation nor dimensional checks establish all required performance. Cross-manufacturer interchangeability remains unproven.

A mature system could reduce repeated uncertainty in planning preparation, compliance evidence and financial assessment. These are conditional possibilities, not approval or funding guarantees. Whole-life assessment should include initial provision for adaptability, maintenance, replacement logistics, disruption and realised reuse.
