ExoSett’s interpretation

Factory production and the capacity to change

Sweden demonstrates that prefabricated housing can operate at national scale, supported by established manufacturing capacity and a regulatory environment that enables industrialised construction. Yet widespread prefabrication has not eliminated housing shortages or guaranteed lower construction costs. Its experience highlights the importance of planning, regulation and the wider conditions of housing delivery alongside factory production.

ExoSett proposes standardisation of building architecture and interfaces while allowing different approaches to pod manufacture. Its separate structural and service frames are intended to accommodate competing suppliers and allow individual pods to be installed, removed and replaced independently. Beyond initial construction, this raises the possibility of extending buildings’ useful lives through reconfiguration—an intended benefit whose practical and economic value remains to be demonstrated.
