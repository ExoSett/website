# Sweden builds houses and apartments like Lego sets. Why don’t we?

## Source

- Slug: `2026-vox-sweden-prefabricated-housing`
- Author: Marina Bolotnikova
- Publication: Future Perfect, Vox; How they built it series
- Publication date: 6 November 2026
- Original URL: https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/509022/sweden-prefabricated-housing-affordability
- Date accessed: 11 November 2026

## Synopsis

Bolotnikova examines Sweden’s extensive use of prefabricated housing and asks what the United States can learn from it. Her reporting shifts attention from factories themselves to the regulatory conditions that allow industrialised construction to develop. Sweden demonstrates that factory-based housing can become mainstream, but its experience does not establish prefabrication as a sufficient solution to housing shortages or high construction costs.

## Main arguments and findings

- Prefabrication covers components, panels and complete room modules; prevalence figures depend on the definition. The article reports an industry estimate that about 90% of new Swedish single-family homes use prefabricated timber systems. It cites national statistics indicating fully prefabricated structural frames in 43% of new multifamily apartments and partial prefabrication in another 46%.
- Sweden’s Million Homes Program helped establish industrialised construction, building on an older tradition of prefabricated timber housing. The American Operation Breakthrough programme did not establish a comparable mainstream market, although it contributed to the development of a national regulatory framework for manufactured housing.
- Interviewees emphasise Sweden’s performance-based and relatively nationally consistent building regulations. These permit different ways of meeting required outcomes and make standardised products more viable across a larger market. Compliance still has to be demonstrated.
- Fragmented US building requirements and rules designed around conventional site construction can impede prefabrication. Zoning can prevent affordable housing types from being built at all, regardless of production method.
- Factory work can shorten schedules and improve working conditions. However, Sweden still faces housing shortages and weak construction productivity. The article reports expert doubt that widespread prefabrication has meaningfully lowered Swedish construction costs.
- American manufactured housing under the national HUD Code offers an existing route to factory-built housing. The article discusses its affordability, zoning and financing obstacles, and changes to permitted building forms. These US policy details are contextual reporting, not guidance for ExoSett.

## Qualifications and limitations

This is reported journalism drawing on interviews, statistics and research, not a controlled cost comparison. Prefabrication includes several distinct methods; figures for prefabricated frames should not be read as figures for complete volumetric homes. National comparisons do not isolate the effect of manufacturing method. The article also discusses a Swedish timber-facade fire and debate about testing, qualifying any suggestion that performance-based regulation guarantees adequate outcomes.

## Relevance to ExoSett

The following is ExoSett’s interpretation, not a finding or endorsement by Vox or the author.

Sweden provides evidence that industrialised housing can operate at national scale, while illustrating that manufacturing sits within a wider delivery system. ExoSett proposes common architecture and interfaces with freedom in pod manufacture. Its separate structural and service frames are intended to enable independent replacement and competing suppliers.

Reconfiguration could extend useful building life, but engineering feasibility, cross-supplier compatibility and economic value require demonstration. Whole-life comparisons must account for the initial provision for adaptability, maintenance, replacement logistics, disruption and actual reuse. This distinction does not imply that all conventional prefabrication ignores adaptation or disassembly.
