## Synopsis

### Meike Schalk (2014)

*The Architecture of Metabolism. Inventing a Culture of Resilience*, Arts 3(2), 279–297. https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0752/3/2/279 ; DOI 10.3390/arts3020279. Full HTML text consulted, including historical context, structural organisation and conclusions. Connects Metabolism to social and cultural questions as well as adaptable physical structures. Its discussion of enduring infrastructure becoming obsolete also informs the article's caution about frame longevity.

### Tatsuyuki Maeda and Yuka Yoshida (2021)

*The real reason why Nakagin Capsule Tower was never metabolized*, Docomomo Journal 65, 118–120. https://docomomojournal.com/index.php/journal/article/view/5 ; DOI 10.52200/65.A.VOJ1OE9B. Full three-page PDF read through text extraction. A preservation participant's account of physical access, finance and collective ownership barriers. Its perspective is explicitly sympathetic to preservation; it predates demolition. Exact historical prices and voting thresholds are omitted from the public article.

### Yuta Osaki, Toshio Otsuki and Yonggeun Lee (2021)

*A Study on the Transition of Nakagin Capsule Tower as One of the Earliest ‘One-Room Mansions’ in Japan*, AIJ Journal of Technology and Design 27(66), 807–811. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijt/27/66/27_807/_article/-char/en ; DOI 10.3130/aijt.27.807. Only the English abstract consulted. It connects use, ownership and management, and identifies obstacles to collective action. The public article states the access limitation explicitly.

### DOCOMOMO Japan

*Sky House*, selection no. 054. https://docomomojapan.com/structure/54/ . English building description consulted. Records the 1958 house, its elevated living area and changeable elements, and subsequent adaptation to family circumstances. No count or detailed chronology of alterations is asserted.

### Alexxa Gotthardt (March 2024)

*A Towering Acquisition*, SFMOMA. https://www.sfmoma.org/read/a-towering-acquisition/ . Full text consulted. Describes the tower's construction, maintenance decline, demolition and preservation of a capsule. The article uses the demolition year, not the precise timing of the final removal; museum and preservation accounts differ on that detail.

### Wikipedia

*Metabolism (architecture)*. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism_(architecture) . Introductory overview and project descriptions consulted. Used for the concise Takara Beautilion, broadcasting-centre and Tower-shaped City overview. The manufacturing parallel is attributed to this overview; it has not been independently established from Kikutake's original proposal.

## Main arguments and findings

The public article separates historical examples from ExoSett's proposed response. A home adapted by one household and a tower with separate unit owners test different conditions. Replacement requires a workable operation and institutions as well as detachable connections.

## Qualifications and limitations

- No claim that the complete Japanese-language paper was reviewed.
- No quotation or detailed claim derived from the unavailable video.
- Other references proposed in the brief were not added merely to lengthen the bibliography. Pernice's landing-page metadata was checked, but a full-text download stalled. Eken and Atun's paper was inspected but not used: its project chronology contains inconsistencies, including Expo dated 1960. O'Neill, Wallace and Saito/Furuya were not relied on.
- Historical photographs omitted: no image licence and attribution package was prepared. Linked building histories provide visual context.
- Water ingress is discussed as a general maintenance concern in ExoSett's lessons, not asserted as a separately verified cause of Nakagin's demolition.
