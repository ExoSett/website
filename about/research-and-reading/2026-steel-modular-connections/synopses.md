## Synopsis

The paper is a structured narrative-comparative review, explicitly not a systematic review or meta-analysis. It reports screening 120 records, retaining 60 as the primary evidence base and supplementing these with contextual references. Nine research-stage steel inter-modular connection systems are assessed against eight criteria: structural capacity, seismic response, energy dissipation, fire performance, constructability, sustainability, reusability and cost impact. VectorBloc is a separate commercial benchmark with a different evidentiary basis, including manufacturer information.

The systems comprise friction rocking, shape-memory-alloy bolts, cruciform plug-in, L-shaped brackets with web cut-outs, H-shaped brackets, mid-depth self-locking plug-in, corrugated web moment frame, post-tensioned rod with friction washer, and omnidirectional through-plate corner connections, alongside VectorBloc. These principally join stacked or adjacent structural modules; the review does not evaluate ExoSett module-to-frame interfaces.

## Main arguments and findings

- No single system is presented as best across all criteria. The authors discuss combining complementary mechanisms and selecting for required performance.
- Sections 3.1 and 6.2 discuss alignment and practical assembly, including cumulative tolerances and restricted site access.
- Sections 3.4 and 3.10 describe sensitivity to geometry, fit and misalignment. Section 3.4 also discusses fretting and limits to reuse after plastic deformation.
- Section 3.7 warns that a self-locking connection can be difficult to dismantle after engagement under load. Assembly speed is therefore distinct from removability.
- Section 3.2 describes designs intended to shift damage away from frame columns into replaceable connection elements. This concerns exceptional structural loading, not simply routine insertion wear.
- Section 3.9 discusses removable post-tensioned rods, controlled tensioning, prestress relaxation, friction-washer wear and restricted anchorage access after cladding.
- Sections 5–7 identify limited fire, long-term durability and building-scale reuse evidence. Section 6.4 stresses disassembly access, traceability and inspection after dismantling.

## Qualifications and limitations

This review is useful for identifying concepts and research questions, but its numerical comparisons should not be reproduced as established design performance. The source studies have not been independently audited for this website entry.

Specific internal issues observed in the supplied PDF:

- Table 3 (pages 7–8) places a post-tensioned-rod rotational stiffness of 45 kNm/rad in the column headed peak load (kN). These are different physical quantities.
- Figure 17 (page 21) describes the highest fire scores as reflecting comprehensive testing, while section 6.4 (page 27) describes partial testing. Section 3.2.3 says friction-rocking fire performance has not been specifically investigated despite its 3/5 fire score.
- Figure 20 labels reuse-cycle counts as estimates, not demonstrated repeated-exchange service lives.
- Some figure source names do not match their numbered references: for example Figure 20 names Minunno [60] and Corfar [85], whereas bibliography entries 60 and 85 are Annan and Peng respectively. Figure 21 names Gatheeshgar [64], whereas entry 64 is Aye.
- Section 2.3 says some cost indices are author estimates derived from qualitative descriptions, rather than consistently measured prices.

The public page consequently uses a restrained qualitative summary and an evidence qualification. It does not reproduce numerical rankings, fire scores, reuse-cycle counts, costs or claims of immediate post-earthquake reoccupation.

## Relevance to ExoSett

The following is ExoSett interpretation, not a conclusion about ExoSett made by the paper's authors.

Shipping-container corner castings, twist locks and associated accessories remain a reasonable working/reference model: a standard interface supports multiple devices and operational functions. ExoSett has never required those particular fittings. The user confirmed that the patent deliberately permits implementation variation; the patent itself was not independently reviewed here. The website's Frame designer and Module designer pages assign coordination and interface responsibilities rather than prescribing a proprietary connector.

The paper broadens the initial set of connection approaches to investigate and reinforces this existing philosophy. Define compatibility and required behaviour consistently while permitting different designs that satisfy them. The different load paths and structural roles of ExoSett modules and their supporting frame prevent direct transfer of reviewed hardware without development and verification.

Lessons concern installation alignment, final restraint and load transfer, realistic tolerances, accessible release after loading, repeated exchange, replaceable wear parts, possible sacrificial elements, inspection, corrosion and fire performance. Wear parts for ordinary exchanges and sacrificial parts for exceptional loading are distinct design options, neither established here as an ExoSett solution.
