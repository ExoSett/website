# The ins and outs of offshore accommodation

## Source

- Slug: `2017-ela-offshore-accommodation`
- Author / organisation: Molly Lempriere, Offshore Technology; interview with Hans Gatzemeier, then described as managing director of ELA Container Offshore
- Publication date: 1 May 2017
- Original URL: https://www.offshore-technology.com/features/featurethe-ins-and-outs-of-offshore-accommodation-5799637/
- Current manufacturer website: https://www.ela-offshore.com/en
- Date accessed: 17 August 2026

## Synopsis

Molly Lempriere’s article examines temporary accommodation for offshore workers through an interview with Hans Gatzemeier of ELA Container Offshore. Offshore vessels and platforms may need to house different numbers of people as projects and work programmes change, while deck space, lifting capacity, safety and exposure to severe weather constrain what can be installed. ELA’s container-based rooms are presented as an alternative to depending entirely on accommodation permanently incorporated into a vessel or installation.

The article describes a range extending beyond sleeping quarters to galleys, offices, mess rooms and laundries. Modules can be lifted onto and removed from rigs, transformer platforms and other offshore facilities. Gatzemeier attributes the growth of temporary living quarters to demand for more flexible crew capacity and faster project mobilisation.

Safety and living standards form two parts of the interview. Gatzemeier says that expectations for certification, comfort, space per person and recreational provision had increased. The article refers to offshore lifting certification, fire and maritime standards, protective coating for harsh weather, protected external HVAC equipment and relatively low tare weight. These are company descriptions rather than independently tested findings reported by the author.

ELA’s standardisation is especially relevant. The article describes a common 20-foot High Cube base conforming to ISO 668 dimensions, with different accommodation solutions fitted within it. Standard dimensions support transport, while common interfaces allow units to be added, removed, exchanged and connected to electricity, fresh water and wastewater systems. The interview presents this combination of a repeatable base and varied uses as a source of flexibility.

ELA’s current website confirms that the 20-foot Offshore Allrounder measures 6,058 × 2,438 × 2,896 mm externally, with an internal height of 2,450 mm. Current variants include multipurpose rooms, living quarters, offices, meeting and dining rooms, locker rooms, recreation and fitness rooms, laundries, drying rooms, small galleys, toilets and sanitary facilities. ELA also currently offers other module types and sizes, including a 33-foot Premium Plus; the whole present range should not therefore be described as 20-foot modules.

Current ELA material describes centrally located connections for electrical power, communications, air conditioning and, when needed, fresh water and wastewater. Modules can be secured horizontally and vertically using corner castings, twistlocks and bolts. Gangway and stairway modules support larger, multi-level accommodation complexes.

## Main arguments and findings

- Offshore projects need accommodation capacity that can respond to changing crew numbers and short mobilisation periods.
- Fully fitted modules can add living, working, sanitary, catering and communal rooms without relying only on accommodation permanently built into a vessel or platform.
- A common 20-foot High Cube base can support different fit-outs while retaining repeatable transport, handling and connection arrangements.
- Offshore modules must address lifting, securing, certification, fire safety, corrosion, services and rapid installation as integrated product requirements.
- Modules can be added, removed or exchanged within larger ELA accommodation complexes.
- ELA’s current catalogue continues to demonstrate multiple room uses based on its standard 20-foot Allrounder, alongside other products.

## Qualifications and limitations

- The source is a trade-publication interview with an ELA executive, not an independent evaluation, comparative study or technical audit.
- Statements about performance, quality, comfort, environmental benefit and flexibility largely describe the manufacturer’s position.
- The article’s company and fleet figures describe 2017 and should not be treated as current. ELA’s present website distinguishes ELA Container Offshore GmbH, founded in 2014, from the longer history of the wider ELA group and its offshore activity.
- The article uses contemporary certification references. Applicable standards, editions and approvals require current specialist verification for any project.
- Offshore certification does not by itself establish compliance with building, housing, accessibility or fire requirements in another setting.
- Dimensional similarity does not demonstrate structural, service-interface or regulatory compatibility with ExoSett.
- ELA now offers 20-foot and 33-foot accommodation products, so the common 20-foot base should not be generalised to its entire range.

## Relevance to ExoSett

The following points are ExoSett interpretations. The article, its author, publisher, interviewee and ELA do not discuss or endorse ExoSett.

- ELA demonstrates that a mature industry already manufactures sophisticated, fully fitted and repeatedly transportable rooms within a standardised envelope.
- The 6,058 × 2,438 × 2,896 mm Allrounder coincides dimensionally with the ISO 668 1CCC 20-foot High Cube envelope used in current ExoSett engineering examples. ELA’s current product page does not itself use the designation `1CCC`; this is an ExoSett comparison.
- A shared physical base supporting many fit-outs is relevant to ExoSett’s intention that independently developed accommodation modules can serve different functions.
- ELA illustrates separation of specialist interests: a manufacturer can concentrate on accommodation products while a wider system supplies their operating context.
- ELA complexes are largely formed by combining and stacking modules with gangway and stairway units. ExoSett instead proposes a persistent accommodation frame and separate service frame into which accommodation modules are inserted.
- Under the ExoSett proposition, an accommodation module need not itself provide the permanent building structure, circulation or shared service routes.
- Manufacturers with capabilities such as ELA’s illustrate relevant established expertise, but no existing ELA product should be described as ExoSett-compatible without design work, testing, certification and agreement with the manufacturer.
