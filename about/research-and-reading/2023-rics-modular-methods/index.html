How modular methods can redress housing crisis

A RICS Property Journal article sets out a property-industry case for modular manufacture while acknowledging recent difficulties in the UK modular sector.

How modular methods can redress housing crisis, by Dr James L. Broun, was published by RICS Property Journal on 15 September 2023. It does not discuss ExoSett. This independent ExoSett discussion does not imply endorsement by the author, RICS, Make UK Modular or the organisations cited.

Property-industry context

Why conventional housebuilding is under pressure

The article connects constrained housing supply with chronic labour and skills shortages, weak construction productivity, limited innovation and the added demands of meeting net-zero requirements. Its central argument is that planning reform alone cannot overcome a housebuilding system that lacks enough workers and has struggled to improve productivity.

This is useful mainstream context for the case for modern methods of construction. It is an industry argument rather than an independent comparative evaluation, and its claims should be read on that basis.

The modular proposition

Factory production can change quality, labour and delivery

The article distinguishes panellised and volumetric modular construction and describes how assembly-line manufacture can use fewer traditional trades, raise productivity and allow more consistent quality control. It also presents faster delivery as a benefit, because modules can be made while groundworks proceed on site, and argues that controlled manufacture can help with airtightness, waste, embodied carbon and future environmental standards.

These are potential advantages of modular manufacture, not evidence specifically for ExoSett. Actual outcomes depend on the system, manufacturer, project, site, procurement and market conditions.

A difficult market

Manufacturing capacity needs a dependable pipeline

The article acknowledges that two major modular producers had recently closed amid inflation, reduced demand and difficult financial markets. It argues that the wider sector remained diverse and capable, but the closures are an important qualification: technical production advantages do not by themselves create a viable market or protect a manufacturer from demand and financing risk.

The article was published in 2023 and describes the sector at that time. It does not provide a retrospective assessment of everything that followed.

The ExoSett distinction

Manufacture the accommodation; preserve the building’s capacity to change

Conventional volumetric modular construction usually combines factory-produced three-dimensional units into the completed building. Modular manufacture addresses how accommodation can be produced efficiently. ExoSett asks a further question: if factory-produced accommodation modules are advantageous, why should their relationship with the building necessarily be permanent?

ExoSett proposes a persistent exoskeletal accommodation frame into which independently replaceable accommodation modules can be installed. It also seeks to separate structure, services and accommodation so that each can change at a different rate and a building can evolve after initial construction. These are ExoSett concepts, not claims made or tested by the RICS article.

Beyond one supplier

Compatible interfaces could support an ecosystem over time

A conventional modular project can depend heavily on one vertically integrated supplier. ExoSett’s longer-term proposition is that defined structural, dimensional and service interfaces could allow compatible modules to be produced, replaced, refurbished or adapted by multiple manufacturers over time.

Such an ecosystem would require durable standards, certification, commercial coordination and a sufficiently large market. ExoSett has not yet demonstrated it. The modular-sector failures noted in the article make this a question worth investigating, not proof that an interoperable model will succeed.

What ExoSett takes from this

Useful background, not evidence for the ExoSett system

The article explains why factory manufacture continues to attract property-industry interest despite commercial setbacks. For ExoSett, it supports asking how controlled production might reduce labour dependency, improve productivity and quality, and accelerate delivery. It does not establish that ExoSett achieves those outcomes.

The further ExoSett question is whether modular production can be combined with long-lived structure and replaceable accommodation.
