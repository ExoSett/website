Research and Reading

Reusable component data and lifecycle records

Reusing a beam requires more than knowing what it was designed to be. We also need to know which physical beam it is, what has happened to it and what evidence supports its next use. Research by Gonçal Costa and colleagues offers a useful perspective on the distinction ExoSett’s 2.0.0 information model makes between reusable definitions and individual lifecycle records.

The authors did not study or validate ExoSett. This discussion relates their independent research to ExoSett’s working 2.0.0 prototype, whose engineering vocabulary and approval processes remain deliberately limited.

Original source

Circular Economy and Sustainability · 11 November 2026

A Reusable Component Data Model for Documenting Building Components in Circular Construction Platforms

Gonçal Costa, Adirane Calvo, Álvaro Sicilia and colleagues · volume 6, article 471 (2026) · DOI: 10.1007/s43615-026-01184-8

Read the open-access paper

The research

Deciding what is worth recording

The researchers develop a practical model for documenting reusable components in existing buildings, where records are incomplete. Their Availability criterion asks whether information can realistically be obtained; Eligibility asks whether it is relevant in context. Explicit selection rules, component applicability and professional review shape the resulting requirements.

The model contains 66 common parameters plus component-specific information. It was applied to six component categories at Barcelona’s Abaceria Central Market. Those categories also informed its development, so this was not independent validation. Recorded values covered roughly 53–63% of applicable parameters; coverage does not establish accuracy or suitability for reuse.

For ExoSett, the useful contribution is the transparent method for choosing information, recognising gaps and examining sources. The paper distinguishes the provenance of parameter definitions from information about individual components. Its Building Legacy implementation combines BIM extraction with complementary records and distinguishes mandatory marketplace information from optional fields. These are documentation requirements, not automatic engineering approval.

Relevance to ExoSett

Class, instance and physical identity

ExoSett’s information-model architecture places component-class definitions within the Building Class Document. Interfaces, requirements and design definitions describe what conforming components are required to be. The prototype represents a bounded subset through definitions, nominal dimensions, dependencies and illustrative constraints. The paper’s category-specific information requirements offer a useful comparison, although describing a component is different from specifying its required performance.

A Component Instance Document follows one identifiable physical item. Manufacture, certificates, inspection, condition, repair, storage, testing and reuse belong to that item’s record and supporting evidence. Two beams of the same class can have different provenance and reuse prospects. A beam may continue to conform to the same class while its condition and history change; retaining its class reference alone does not prove continued conformity.

The prototype preserves an external identity’s scheme, issuer and value. ExoSett’s standards investigation favours international and industry identification practice over proprietary serials, considering ISO/IEC 15459-4 and ISO/IEC 8506 alongside the separate reference-designation role of ISO 81346-12. These are ExoSett’s investigations, not standards endorsed by Costa and colleagues or a claim of implemented conformance.

Relevance to ExoSett

Components move; positions remain

Imagine a beam installed in one building, removed to storage, inspected and later installed in another. Its physical identity persists. The first position can receive a replacement, while the original beam occupies a different position elsewhere. A position’s identity and its occupant’s identity must therefore remain separate; positions can themselves be modified or retired through explicit continuity records.

The Building Instance Document records the adopted design and the component revision supporting each occupied position. The component record identifies its installed building and position. Installation, removal and replacement change both accounts of deployment. The 2.0.0 publication checks reconcile these assignments, including the removed and replacement items.

ExoSett information model

History as immutable revisions

Each issued revision preserves an account of a state. New inspection findings, repair, relocation, installation, removal or an authorised correction should create a new revision, retaining earlier assertions. The 2.0.0 format distinguishes recording, observation and effective times: learning something today does not mean it happened today. Corrections identify earlier revisions, a reason and evidence; the prototype does not authenticate the authority making them.

A publication set coordinates exact building and component revisions against an accepted baseline, with their class dependencies resolved separately. Their identities and histories remain independent. A later inspection can advance a component record while the building retains the revision supporting its installation, provided deployment and class qualification still agree. A remove–repair–reinstall sequence needs intermediate records or work evidence even when the final location is unchanged.

Known, unknown and evidenced

After several owners or buildings, a complete history cannot be assumed. Required and optional describe information obligations; unknown, unavailable and not applicable describe different reasons for missing values. The prototype already permits explicit unknown deployment, occupancy and effective time. It does not yet provide a general property-level vocabulary for these distinctions, and condition remains descriptive text.

A manufacturer’s certificate, an inference from an old BIM model, an inspection measurement and someone’s recollection offer different evidence. Existing links identify an external evidence artifact, its subject and role, allowing certificates and reports to remain outside the core XML. That is a useful foundation, but does not establish each value’s method, uncertainty or reliability. File integrity and resolved references cannot establish the truth or continuing validity of a certificate.

ExoSett should examine minimum evidence by lifecycle stage: manufacture, installation, continued use, removal, sale and reuse can require different information. Marketplace completeness is not a sufficient test for reinstalling a structural component. Inspection and recertification requirements need an agreed professional process, supported by records.

Questions for ExoSett

What should the next revision address?

  1. Which properties define a component class, and which record an individual item’s measured condition, with explicit unknown, unavailable or not-applicable values?
  2. How should individual assertions identify their sources, measurement or inference methods and uncertainty, using external certificates, inspection reports and test results where appropriate?
  3. What minimum evidence and responsible assessment should each lifecycle stage require, especially before a used structural component is installed again?
  4. Which changes require component-only revisions, and which require coordinated building revisions through a publication set, including intermediate repair and removal records?

These questions complement the physical interfaces discussed in Demountable connections and Design for disassembly: reusable construction needs both recoverable components and usable evidence about them.
